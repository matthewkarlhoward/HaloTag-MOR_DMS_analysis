# SR-17018 TruPath (Gi1 BRET) — local copy

Self-contained copy of every SR-17018 TruPath file, so this analysis no longer needs
`MKH_DRIVE` mounted. Everything in `raw/` was copied verbatim (mtimes preserved); the
CSVs beside this README are regenerated by `extract_sr17018_trupath.py`, which reads
only from `raw/`.

**Bottom line: use 42.4 ± 6.8% of DAMGO at 100 µM**, from the 20260117 plates with the 100 µM point measured against DAMGO's baseline rather than SR-17018's own (`common_baseline_20260117.py`). That run is
the published 14-ligand panel's own plate set, where buprenorphine reproduces its
published value to within 3.5 points and DAMGO has a full assay window. The later
20260502 run gives 126% at the same nominal dose, but there DAMGO's window is only ~47%
of normal and the donor channel collapses in SR-17018's top-dose wells.

> **Correction (2026-10-05).** An earlier pass over the 20260117 data in this file called
> it "flat and slightly the wrong direction". That was wrong: the dose axis was read
> backwards. In these tables **1e-4 is the most concentrated point and 1e-13/1e-14 is the
> baseline**, so the drop at 1e-4 is in the correct signal-down direction. See
> `analyze_20260117.py`.

## Two attempts

**20260117 — the usable run.** These are the published panel's own plates. SR-17018 was
on drug plate 2 (rows A/B/O/P of `20260117_mor_trupath_processing.xlsx`), in two series
sharing the same 10 plate/read columns P1R1..P4R2 × 2 technical reps: `SR17018 comp`
(undiluted stock, top dose 100 µM) and `SR17018(dil) comp` (10× diluted, top dose 10 µM).

**Where the published numbers actually come from.** The 14 published Emax values are
produced by the house recipe applied to the `Composite` sheet — 6 replicates per ligand,
which is 3 plate/reads × 2 technical reps, **hand-picked per ligand** ("read at cycles 3/4
and chose best looking ones", `TRUPATH_exp_notes.txt`). Running the recipe on `Composite`
reproduces all 14 to **0.00 points**, verified by `analyze_20260117.py`.

**SR-17018 is not in `Composite`.** It was dropped from the published analysis, so it never
got that per-ligand read selection. To place it we have to rebuild it from `SR17018 comp`
(all 10 plate/reads × 2 reps) and anchor it to the published DAMGO (activity 0.4619 at
10<sup>-5</sup>).

| selection | % DAMGO | SEM | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| DAMGO's own three reads (P2R3+P3R2+P4R2) | **36.9** | 11.3 | 6 |
| all ten reads | 35.0 | 8.5 | 20 |
| SR17018(dil), all ten reads | ~9 | — | 20 |

**The read-to-read spread is the real uncertainty.** Per plate/read SR-17018 ranges from
0% to 78%. That is not unique to it — over the same ten reads buprenorphine spans 17–89%,
PZM21 46–99% and DAMGO 79–122%. These plates are simply noisy read to read, which is
exactly why three were hand-picked per ligand for the published table. SR-17018 is at the
noisy end of a noisy set, and no one ever made that selection for it.

Prism does flag its own 4-parameter fits of these tables as unstable, which is fair:
the curve has not saturated at the top dose, so an EC50 is not identifiable. The
peak-of-mean Emax does not depend on a fit.

### This run passes the donor QC that 20260502 failed

`donor_check_20260117.py`, using the raw CLARIOstar reads now copied into `raw/`:

| ligand | top dose | donor@top | donor@mid | change | r(donor,ratio)@top |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SR17018 | 10<sup>-4</sup> | 1,802 | 2,116 | **−15%** | −0.52 |
| SR17018(dil) | 10<sup>-5</sup> | 1,820 | 2,839 | −36% | −0.69 |
| DAMGO | 10<sup>-4</sup> | 4,400 | 6,259 | −30% | −0.58 |
| Buprenorphine | 10<sup>-4</sup> | 1,026 | 5,344 | −81% | −0.44 |

SR-17018 has the **smallest** donor change of the four at its own top dose, and its
donor/ratio coupling there (−0.52) is unremarkable beside DAMGO's (−0.58). Contrast
20260502, where SR-17018 lost 46% of its donor while DAMGO and buprenorphine lost
nothing, and its coupling jumped to +0.77 against a +0.32 background. Buprenorphine's
−81% here does not matter because its peak is at 10<sup>-6</sup>, not the top dose.

**Sensitivity, not a correction.** The BRET ratio is mildly, negatively coupled to donor
on these plates, and the baseline wells sit at the low-donor end of the column gradient,
which inflates every ligand's apparent drop. Estimating the slope per ligand from its own
pharmacologically flat dilute doses and subtracting gives SR-17018 28.1%. But the same
correction moves buprenorphine from 56.7% to 49.3%, i.e. *further* from its published
60.2%, so the correction is not validated and the published panel is uncorrected anyway.
Quote the uncorrected value for consistency with the panel, and treat **~28–37%** as the honest band.

**Caveat that stands:** `TRUPATH_exp_notes.txt` records *"SR17018 crashed out in HBSS,
even when diluted 10x in DMS first."* The free concentration at a nominal 100 µM is
therefore unknown, so 37.9% is an efficacy at an uncertain concentration rather than at
100 µM of dissolved compound. 300 µM was later found soluble in 10 mM HEPES pH 7.5 + 1%
Tween 80; that condition was never assayed. Raw CLARIOstar reads for this run are still
on MKH_DRIVE (`drive2/Clariostar/20260117_mkh_mor_plate*.xlsx`) and were **not** copied
here, so the donor-collapse check done for 20260502 cannot be repeated offline.

**20260502 — ran, but the only responding point is an artifact.** 12 replicates per dose,
10<sup>-4</sup>–10<sup>-14</sup> M plus vehicle. Prism's own 3-parameter fits
(`sr17018_trupath_prism_fits.csv`):

| | Bottom (vehicle) | Top | LogEC50 | Span | R² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DAMGO | 0.1991 | 0.1576 | −7.694 (20 nM) | −0.0416 | 0.644 |
| Buprenorphine | 0.1868 | Unstable | Unstable | Unstable | 0.000 |
| SR17018 | 0.2040 | 0.1237 | −4.356 (44 µM) | −0.0803 | 0.582 |

SR-17018's whole response is the single 10<sup>-4</sup> M point (0.1481 ± 0.0053, n=12)
against its own plateau of 0.2059 ± 0.0012 (n=96). The curve never saturates, so `Top` is
unconstrained — its 95% CI runs to −0.51, i.e. negative — and the −0.0803 Span is
extrapolation, not a measured Emax.

Taking that point at face value puts SR-17018 at **135–140% of DAMGO's maximum**
(135% off each ligand's own vehicle, 140% off each ligand's own low-dose plateau, 135%
from Prism's fitted Bottom against DAMGO's Span). Exceeding DAMGO is itself the tell.

## QC: why the top point is an artifact

`sr17018_trupath_qc.csv`. Donor luminescence (410 nm) collapses in SR-17018's top-dose
wells and nowhere else:

| ligand | top dose donor410 | its own mid-dose donor410 | loss | r(donor, ratio) at top |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DAMGO | 20,365 | 21,746 | −6% | +0.56 |
| Buprenorphine | 18,569 | 21,452 | −13% | +0.57 |
| **SR17018** | **9,627** | 17,686 | **−46%** | **+0.77** |

DAMGO and buprenorphine occupy the same columns 1–2 and lose essentially nothing, so this
is not a plate-edge effect. And within SR-17018's top-dose wells the BRET ratio tracks
donor counts at r = +0.77, against +0.32–0.38 elsewhere in its own rows, while plate-wide
donor vs ratio is flat (r = +0.10, quintile means 0.188–0.194). The worse the well, the
larger the apparent response — the signature of precipitation, quench, or acute toxicity
at 100 µM, not receptor activation.

One genuine edge effect does exist and is unrelated: the vehicle columns 23–24 have low
donor counts for all three ligands (8,400–10,800). It does not touch the dose–response.

## % DAMGO for the single point, by the house recipe

`emax_pct_damgo.py` -> `sr17018_emax_pct_damgo.csv`. Applies the published TruPath
recipe ([[reference_trupath_emax_normalization]]): per replicate divide by its own
no-drug baseline, activity = 1 - norm, Emax = peak of the per-dose **mean** activity,
then / DAMGO peak x 100. On this plate each row carries two replicate series (the odd
and even column of each dose pair), giving 6 DAMGO and 12 SR-17018 replicates.

**SR-17018 = 132.5% of DAMGO (SEM 12.7), peak at 10<sup>-4</sup> M**, on the house
baseline (own 10<sup>-13</sup>). DAMGO's own peak falls at 10<sup>-5</sup> M.

Sensitivity to the baseline choice, which this plate does not pin down:

| per-replicate baseline | SR-17018 % DAMGO | buprenorphine % DAMGO |
|---|---|---|
| own 10<sup>-13</sup> (house recipe) | **132.5 ± 12.7** | 33.9 |
| own 10<sup>-14</sup> | 128.4 ± 12.3 | 45.0 |
| own vehicle well | 126.4 ± 13.0 | 76.1 |
| own 10<sup>-7</sup>–10<sup>-14</sup> mean | 146.4 ± 11.5 | 42.4 |

A plain difference-of-means (no per-replicate normalisation) agrees: 127–155%.
So the headline is robust at **~130–140% of DAMGO, and firmly above 100%**.

### Why it comes out over 100%, and why that is not a real finding

Two things, neither of them SR-17018 being a super-agonist.

**1. It is not statistically above 100%.** On the vehicle baseline, per-replicate peak
activity is 0.2546 ± 0.0261 (n=12) for SR-17018 against 0.2014 ± 0.0179 (n=6) for DAMGO.
Welch t = 1.68, df = 16, so the **95% CI on % DAMGO is 95–158%**. The point estimate sits
above 100 but the interval includes it. Equivalently, the BRET floor SR-17018 reaches
(0.1481 ± 0.0053) is only 1.2 SEM below DAMGO's (0.1554 ± 0.0035) — the two are
indistinguishable. The honest reading is "reaches DAMGO's maximum", not "exceeds it".

**2. DAMGO's window on this plate is about a third of normal.** Peak activity for DAMGO
here is 0.2014, against ~0.651 recomputed from the published `norm` tab
(`trupath_vs_camp_efficacy/trupath_wt_drc_ligands.xlsx`). Buprenorphine shows the same
compression (0.1532 here vs ~0.434). DAMGO's potency is down too: logEC50 −7.694 here vs
−8.427 published. So the denominator is a weak DAMGO, which inflates every ratio on the
plate and widens its error.

(The ~0.651 is approximate: a from-scratch recompute of the published tab returns
Bup/DAMGO = 66.7% against the published 60.2%, and Nalbuphine 34.8% against 47.0%, because
that tab is already averaged and the exact recipe is per-replicate. The 3.2-fold window
difference is far larger than that slop.)

### Normalisation options, and what each gives

| approach | SR-17018 | note |
|---|---|---|
| own vehicle well | **126% (95% CI 95–158)** | preferred here; vehicle is the cleanest same-row anchor |
| own 10<sup>-13</sup> (house recipe) | 132.5 ± 12.7 | matches the published panel's recipe |
| own 10<sup>-14</sup> | 128.4 ± 12.3 | |
| own 10<sup>-7</sup>–10<sup>-14</sup> mean | 146.4 ± 11.5 | highest, because SR-17018's plateau is the highest of the three |
| raw ΔBRET, no DAMGO ratio | 0.051 ± 0.005 vs DAMGO 0.040 ± 0.008 | implies no efficacy claim; most defensible if the point must be shown |
| anchored to DAMGO's *normal* window | ~39% | assumes the plate's scale transfers, which the buprenorphine control says it does not |
| row-matched to a full agonist | not possible | DAMGO is in rows A/F/K, SR-17018 in D/E/I/J/N/O — no DAMGO in its rows. This is the design gap |
| fraction of maximal achievable dissociation | not possible | no max-dissociation control on the plate (row P is cell-free blanks) |

Note DAMGO's peak is at 10<sup>-5</sup>, not 10<sup>-4</sup>; using DAMGO's own
10<sup>-4</sup> point as the denominator instead gives 136%.

### The cross-plate control fails

Buprenorphine ran on both this plate and the published 14-ligand panel, where it is
**60.2% DAMGO**. Here it comes out anywhere from **31% to 82%** depending only on which
baseline is used, because its dilute end never settles: doses 10<sup>-5</sup>–10<sup>-14</sup>
wander between 0.1818 and 0.1912 with no plateau, and its vehicle wells (0.2043) sit ~10%
above every one of its dilute doses.

That means this plate cannot reproduce a known Emax, so an SR-17018 value from it is not
on the same footing as the published panel. The ~132% is internally matched (same plate,
same read, same DAMGO) but should not be dropped into the 14-ligand figure as if it were
cross-comparable.

### The dilute end is flat; the top three doses are not

Per-dose mean activity as % of the DAMGO peak, baseline 10<sup>-13</sup>:

| dose | SR-17018 | DAMGO |
|---|---|---|
| 10<sup>-4</sup> | 132.5 | 94.6 |
| 10<sup>-5</sup> | 33.4 | 100.0 |
| 10<sup>-6</sup> | 50.4 | 90.4 |
| 10<sup>-7</sup> | 12.3 | 79.1 |
| 10<sup>-8</sup> and below | −5 to +6 | 32.1 down to ~0 |

Correcting an earlier read of this data: SR-17018 is **not** flat from 10<sup>-5</sup> down.
10<sup>-5</sup> (0.1920) and 10<sup>-6</sup> (0.1841) both sit significantly below its
plateau of ~0.2059 (SEM ~0.003, so 3–7 SEM), i.e. roughly 33% and 50% of DAMGO. But the
order is inverted — 10<sup>-6</sup> reads *more* active than 10<sup>-5</sup> — so this is
not a dose–response. Donor counts are collapsed at 10<sup>-4</sup> and 10<sup>-5</sup>
(9,627 and 9,924, columns 1–4) but normal at 10<sup>-6</sup> (16,382), so donor loss does
not explain the 10<sup>-6</sup> dip either. Only 10<sup>-7</sup> and below is a clean
baseline.

## Buprenorphine is mislabelled by one log unit

Validating the plate map against the `.prism` value-for-value turned up a data-entry bug:
**the buprenorphine series is pasted one row too low.** Every buprenorphine dose label in
the `.prism` is one log unit too dilute (labelled 10<sup>-5</sup> is really
10<sup>-4</sup>, and so on), and its vehicle row holds 24 values because the
10<sup>-14</sup> point was pushed into it. Full audit in
`sr17018_trupath_prism_label_check.csv`.

DAMGO and SR-17018 match their labels exactly, so nothing above is affected. Use the raw
mapping (`sr17018_trupath_wells.csv`, `sr17018_trupath_dose_summary.csv`) rather than the
`.prism` dose labels if you ever revisit buprenorphine here.

## Plate map

Reverse engineered from the raw reads, then validated against the `.prism` — 24 of 35
ligand × dose rows match exactly, and the 11 that do not are the buprenorphine offset above.

- **Rows** — DAMGO A/F/K, buprenorphine B/C/G/H/L/M, SR-17018 D/E/I/J/N/O, row P blanks
- **Columns** — dose pairs, 1–2 = 10<sup>-4</sup> M through 21–22 = 10<sup>-14</sup> M, 23–24 = vehicle

## If this gets repeated

Use the untested 10 mM HEPES pH 7.5 + 1% Tween 80 condition, add points between
10<sup>-5</sup> and 10<sup>-4</sup> M to constrain the top of the curve, and record
donor counts per well so precipitation is separable from activation at read time.
